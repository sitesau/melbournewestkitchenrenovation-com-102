The first decision is whether a kitchen needs a full rebuild or a simpler update. A makeover keeps the existing footprint and focuses on doors, benchtops and splashbacks. This is usually faster and cheaper. A rebuild changes the layout, storage or workflow and may involve structural work. Timeline and Process

A standard full renovation usually runs around 6 to 12 weeks from contract to handover. This timeline is useful because it splits the job into real stages. Cabinetry and benchtop fabrication often takes 4 to 6 weeks. The on site stage usually takes 2 to 4 weeks for demolition, install, plumbing, electrical and finishing. A makeover is generally quicker. The more practical risk is starting fabrication before layout, materials and appliance choices are settled. If you want the program to stay on track, ask when selections must be locked in, when templates are taken for benchtops and what would restart fabrication or installation timing. The handover standard is also stated clearly, cleanup, a working kitchen, and warranty and compliance paperwork in writing.

Materials and Compliance

Benchtop decisions now involve a clear compliance check. Engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. The business states that current options include porcelain and sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber and compliant low silica alternatives. That makes early selection more useful than late comparison. Ask to see the benchtop choices that fit your layout, sink plan and finish level, then price them side by side. If you are weighing appearance against a simpler program, it helps to decide that before cabinetry is finalised. The site also says projects run with the right permits and registered plumbing and electrical trades in line with Victorian building requirements.

Contract and Paperwork

For domestic building work over $10,000 in Victoria, a written major domestic building contract is required under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. The contract sets out the scope, price, payment stages and variations process. You can confirm the current threshold with Consumer Affairs Victoria and the building regulator. The business says every project starts with a free in home measure up and an itemised fixed scope written quote. That makes the quote more than a price tag. It is the main document for checking what has actually been allowed for before work starts. Compare whether demolition, rough in work, cabinetry, benchtops, splashback, plumbing, electrical, appliances and finishing are included. Make sure the contract explains payment stages and the process for approving changes.
